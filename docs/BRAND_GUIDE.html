Fuel & Form
Brand & UX Guide · v3 · 2026-08-10

A personal nutrition and training tracker. Monochrome, typographically extreme, one warm accent that only ever means now.

Governing principle Divergence from plan should become data rather than guilt. docs/PRD.md
01

Four rules that carry the whole design

No gradients, no textures, no shadows outside sheets. What makes it feel designed instead of generic is scale, restraint, and two signature graphics.

01 — Scale

7× contrast, minimum

76px display against a 10.5px label. Anything in between is where designs go to look ordinary — mid-sized type reads as a default, never a decision.

02 — Accent

Umber means “now”

Not brand colour, not actions, not emphasis. The NOW marker, today's dot, and the swap tint. One umber element per screen and it always says: you are here.

03 — Actions

Ink, not colour

Primary buttons and the active tab are near-black in light, near-white in dark. Nothing competes with the accent, and the screen stays monochrome.

04 — Graphics

Two devices, two time-scales

The day ruler for intraday, the dot grid for weeks. Split this way they never appear as alternatives to each other on the same screen.

02

The two signature graphics

Both are abstractions of data the app already has — neither is decoration, and neither needs colour to work.

Day ruler · intraday
NOW
0612 1822
Logged Skipped Upcoming

Eight slots across a 06:00–22:00 span. Filled bars are logged, hatched are skipped, hairlines are still to come. The umber tick and pill mark the present. The whole day's shape in 40px of vertical space — and it answers “where am I?” before a single word is read.

Dot grid · multi-week
MTWTFSS

Six weeks of training adherence, one row per week. Filled is done, ring is skipped, small dots are walk-only weekends. Today is the single umber dot. The PRD wants adherence visible without a score — this shows the pattern and refuses to grade it.

Why both, and why they never collide. They operate at different resolutions: the ruler is one day at hour precision, the grid is six weeks at day precision. The ruler lives on /; the grid lives on Training and Weight. No screen shows both, so neither has to compete to be the memorable thing.

04

Scale is the design

System stack, no webfont — SF Pro on the phone this is built for, and no blocking request in the PRD's sub-1.5-second budget. What does the work is the ratio between the top and the bottom of this table.

The ratio is the rule: 76 ÷ 10.5 ≈ 7.2×. Everything between Value and Micro is deliberately empty. Mid-sized type is what makes an interface read as a default rather than a decision — the references all avoid it, and so does this.

Two exceptions that outrank the aesthetic. Body stays at 17px, because the dominant case is a phone at arm's length, one-handed, mid-cook — legibility beats refinement on the one screen the app exists for. And protein stays emphasised by weight rather than colour, because colour is spoken for.

05

Monochrome, plus one meaning

Warm stone neutrals carry the entire interface. Umber is not the brand colour — it is the present tense, and it appears once per screen.

Brown, not amber — and it simplified the system. The amber this started from measured 2.7:1 on white: it failed AA as text and missed even the 3:1 threshold for UI components, so it needed a second darker token for ink plus a rule about which to use where. Umber clears 5.8:1, so fill and ink are one value. Confining it to “now” then removed the last judgement call — there is never a question of whether something should be accented.

06

Flat, and the details that replace depth

No gradients. No textures. No shadows outside sheets. Distinctiveness comes from these instead.

Hatching
Untracked · skipped · flexible

Hard-edged two-tone stripes at 45°. A pattern, not a texture — no blending, no opacity ramp, no noise. It marks the absence of data without implying failure.

Line motifs

One per meal type, 1.6px stroke, currentColor so a single set works on ink and stone in both modes. Eight marks cover the entire library — and there is no photography to shoot, licence or ship.

Crop marks
Day complete

Registration marks from print. They appear on the day-complete summary and nowhere else — the day is a finished page, and a device used once keeps its meaning.

Slash metadata
612 kcal · 25 min · serves 1 Chicken & Rice → Chilli · +21 kcal 98 of 148 g

A leading slash in tertiary grey marks every secondary fact. It replaces the parentheses, dashes and colons that make interface copy look unconsidered, and it costs one character.

Also deliberately absent: icons that repeat their own label, filled status pills, card borders, elevation, and any second accent. The only shadow in the system sits under a sheet, because a sheet genuinely floats.

07

Voice

A neutral instrument. It reports; it does not congratulate, scold, or motivate. No exclamation marks anywhere. The right-hand column is the default every fitness app ships with.

08

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA, measured rather than assumed. Where restraint and contrast conflict, contrast wins and the hairline gets darker.

Contrast

≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 for large text and every control, tick, dot and hairline that carries meaning.

Never colour alone

The ruler encodes status as fill, hatch and hairline; the dot grid as solid, ring and size. Both survive greyscale — and both already are greyscale.

Signature graphics

The ruler and the grid each carry an accessible summary plus a data table. A mark on a screen is not the data.

Micro labels

10.5px is small. It is permitted only for labels whose value sits adjacent at 22px or more, never for standalone information, and it scales with Dynamic Type.

Focus

2px umber ring, 2px offset, on every interactive element in both modes. Never removed.

Touch

44×44px minimum. Icon-only tabs carry an aria-label; the active tab shows its label as text.
